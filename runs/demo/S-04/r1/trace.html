Step 1

Step 1. The simulated Federal Household Benefit Service (FHBS) asks the simulated Dubai Population Records Office (DPRO) to share one resident's household record for an eligibility pre-check. The record holds personal data. The DPRO reports that nobody has asked this resident for consent to exchange the record with another government entity. May the DPRO share the record?

Policy and legal adviser Escalated to a person

U16 Section 5.2 states that personal information 'will be exchanged effectively between government authorities with the consent of the customer', which points against sharing without consent. However, U16 is framed as a basic guideline with mandatory-sounding but non-statutory wording, and U01 excludes government entities from its consent rules, so the sources do not clearly settle whether a binding consent requirement applies to this DPRO-to-FHBS exchange.

“This information will be exchanged effectively between government authorities with the consent of the customer and while maintaining confidentiality and privacy of the data.”

Step 2. The resident has now given written consent, recorded by the DPRO, to exchange this record with the FHBS for the eligibility pre-check. The consent names that purpose. May the DPRO share the record now?

Policy and legal adviser Escalated to a person

The recorded written consent now meets the consent condition in U16 Section 5.2 and the purpose-naming element of U12 Article 23(1), but U12 governs exchanges with Government Entities in the Emirate and FHBS is a federal body. U01 Article 2 excludes government data, so the passages do not clearly establish that a binding rule authorises this federal exchange.
